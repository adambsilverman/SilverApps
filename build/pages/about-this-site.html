About this site

Who makes it

SilverApps is written and maintained by Adam Silverman (opens in a new tab), a composer who teaches music theory.

The notation

I typeset the music notation on this site. The staves inside the apps are drawn as you work, with VexFlow; the score images in the lessons I engraved myself. The few exceptions, such as a medieval chant manuscript and a page of published sheet music, are credited where they appear.

Most of what you'll see is public-domain music: folk songs, hymns, carols and classical repertoire whose copyrights have expired. The exceptions are short excerpts of copyrighted songs, typeset here only so they can be analyzed. Song lyrics are left out of typed chord charts.

The recordings

Some audio is mine: my own performances, my voice, and examples I recorded for a particular lesson. Other clips are short excerpts of commercial recordings. Videos from YouTube are embedded from their published sources rather than copied here.

Fair use

Short excerpts of copyrighted music, whether a few bars of a score or a few seconds of a recording, are included under fair use, 17 U.S.C. § 107. You'll see a note under each one. All rights remain with the artists and copyright holders.

The claim rests on how the excerpts are used. The site is free and nonprofit, and it exists for teaching. Each excerpt is the smallest passage that shows the point being taught, and it sits next to commentary and analysis of that passage. None of them can stand in for the song, the recording or the published sheet music. If you hold rights to something here and object to its use, contact me through adambsilverman.com (opens in a new tab) and I'll take it down.

Privacy

The site counts visits with Umami: which pages are opened, and the city, region and country they're opened from. No names, no email addresses, no cookies. Your practice settings and progress stay in your own browser.
