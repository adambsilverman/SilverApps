Key ideas
  1. Borrowed chords come from the parallel minor: i, ii°, iiø⁷, iv, v, ♭III, ♭VI, ♭VII (and vii°⁷) in a major key. A major chord made minor → mixture; a minor chord made major → secondary dominant. Picardy third: I at the end of a minor-key passage.
  2. Neapolitan: a major triad on ♭2̂, usually ♭II⁶ (its 3rd, 4̂, in the bass; root and 5th above, one doubled). It moves to V, often through i⁶₄ or vii°⁷/V.
  3. Augmented sixths: le (♭6̂) in the bass, fi (♯4̂) above, both resolving out to sol. Italian adds do; French adds do and re; German adds do and me.
  4. German sixth vs. V⁷: same sound; fi (raised 4̂) goes up to V, sol♭ (a 7th) goes down to ♭II.
  5. Chromatic mediants: same quality, roots a M3 or m3 apart, not in one key, sharing a common tone.
