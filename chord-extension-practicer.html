How chords are spelled here
  1. Chords stack in thirds from the root: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13. Each degree takes the next letter in the stack, so the 9th of F♯ is some kind of G and the ♯11 is some kind of B. That is why F♯m13(♭9/♯11) needs B♯ and not C.
  2. The biggest number in the symbol, including an altered one in parentheses, brings every chord tone below it. C9 is C E G B♭ D, and a ♯11 always comes with a 9th, so the chord is written CΔ9(♯11), not CΔ7(♯11).
  3. That includes the 11 on every 13th chord, major and dominant too: C13 is C E G B♭ D F A, a full stack of seven thirds.
  4. Alterations in parentheses replace the natural tone of the same degree: C13(♭9) has D♭ instead of D, and C13(♯11) has F♯ instead of F.
  5. Answers are graded on spelling, not just sound. A right pitch on the wrong letter is marked in amber.
