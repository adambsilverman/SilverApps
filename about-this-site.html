About this site

Who makes it

SilverApps is written and maintained by Adam Silverman (opens in a new tab), a composer who teaches music theory. All of the teaching ideas are his. The site was coded largely with Anthropic's Claude, which also generated some of the text.

The notation

Silverman typeset the music notation on this site. The staves inside the apps are drawn as you work, with VexFlow; the score images in the lessons he engraved himself. The few exceptions, such as a medieval chant manuscript and a page of published sheet music, are credited where they appear.

Most of what you'll see is public-domain music: folk songs, hymns, carols and classical repertoire whose copyrights have expired. The exceptions are short excerpts of copyrighted songs, typeset here only so they can be analyzed. The pop song transcriptions are Silverman's.

The recordings

Some audio was created specifically for a particular lesson; other audio is generated adaptively. The rest are short excerpts of commercial recordings. Videos from YouTube are embedded from their published sources rather than copied here.

Fair use

Short excerpts of copyrighted music, whether a few bars of a score or a few seconds of a recording, are included under fair use, 17 U.S.C. § 107. You'll see a note under each one. All rights remain with the artists and copyright holders.

The claim rests on how the excerpts are used. The site is free and nonprofit, and it exists for teaching. Each excerpt is the smallest passage that shows the point being taught, and it sits next to commentary and analysis of that passage. None of them can stand in for the song, the recording or the published sheet music. If you hold rights to something here and object to its use, contact Silverman through adambsilverman.com (opens in a new tab) and it will be taken down.

Using SilverApps

Silverman's apps, lessons, text, graphics and code are licensed under Creative Commons BY-NC-SA 4.0 (opens in a new tab). Teach with them, adapt them, put them on your own site. Keep the credit, don't sell them, and share your version under the same license. That license doesn't cover the excerpts of other people's music described above, or the open-source pieces the site is built on (VexFlow and its Bravura font, and the fonts, each under its own license).

Privacy

The site counts visits with Umami: which pages are opened, and the city, region and country they're opened from. No names, no email addresses, no cookies. Your practice settings and progress stay in your own browser.
