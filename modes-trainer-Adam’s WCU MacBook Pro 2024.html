How modes work here
  1. Diatonic modes are seven-note scales that start on each degree of a major scale. Learn each one by how it differs from major or natural minor: Dorian is minor ♯6, Mixolydian is major ♭7.
  2. Spelling. Seven-note modes use every letter once, so D Dorian is D E F G A B C. A right pitch on the wrong letter is marked in amber. For the blues scale, the added tone may be spelled ♯4 or ♭5. Whole-tone and octatonic scales are graded by sound, so any enharmonic spelling counts.
  3. PADMIL. Phrygian → Aeolian → Dorian → Mixolydian → Ionian → Lydian. Each step to the right raises one degree by a half step, which adds a sharp or removes a flat. Locrian sits to the left of Phrygian.
  4. Finding a modal key signature. Start from the tonic's major key signature, then: Lydian one sharp more; Mixolydian one flat more; Dorian two flats more; Aeolian three; Phrygian four; Locrian five. A Mixolydian = A major (3♯) minus one sharp = 2♯.
  5. Identifying a mode by ear: find the tonic, decide whether the third above it is major or minor, then find the degree that differs from major or natural minor.
